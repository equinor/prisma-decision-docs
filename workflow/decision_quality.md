# Decision Quality assessment

## Purpose

Evaluate and document whether the decision process has been conducted in a way that supports making a high-quality decision.
The objective is not to judge whether the outcome will be successful, but whether the decision is based on the best available understanding of the problem.

## Concept

see [Decision quality](../domain/decision_quality.md)


## Methodological guidance

To guide the evaluation, we address below the 6 elements with

- Key question
- Typical concerns
- Prompt
- Signs of a low score

The typical concerns section shouldn't be used as a check list but is rather a preparation material,
a mental reprensation, for the facilitator. The prompt gathers elements which should be discussed and addressed by the team, and a score should only be given after a discussion was held. Finally, the signs of low score helps calibrating the score.


### Appropriate Frame 

- Key question: 
  Are we solving the right decision problem? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Is the decision question clearly stated? 
  - Have we defined what success looks like? 
  - Are the decision-makers identified? 
  - Is the scope too narrow or too broad? 
  - Have we challenged underlying assumptions? 
  - Are we addressing the symptom rather than the root issue? 
  - Are important stakeholders missing? 
  - Have we considered the "do nothing" case? 
- Prompts
  - What decision are we actually making?"
  - If we solve this perfectly, what outcome are we trying to achieve?"
  - Could we be framing the problem incorrectly?"
- Signs of a low score 
  - Frequent disagreement about the objective. 
  - Different participants discussing different decisions. 
  - Unclear ownership or decision authority. 

### Creative, Doable Alternatives

- Key question: 
  Do we have enough good options to choose from? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Are we comparing only variations of the same idea? 
  - Has the team become attached to a preferred option? 
  - Have disruptive or unconventional alternatives been considered? 
  - Have external benchmarks been reviewed? 
  - Is "status quo" included? 
  - Have hybrid solutions been considered? 
  - Are implementation issues well understood?
- Prompts 
  - What options have we not yet considered?"
  - If our preferred solution was unavailable, what would we do?
  - What would a competitor do?"
- Signs of a low score 
  - Only one realistic option exists. 
  - Alternatives differ only marginally. 
  - Decision feels predetermined. 

### Meaningful, Reliable Information 

- Key question: 
  Do we know what we need to know? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Are key uncertainties understood? 
  - Is critical data missing?
  - Are assumptions evidence-based? 
  - Is information current? 
  - Are sources trustworthy? 
  - Have experts been consulted? 
  - Are there significant unknowns that could change the decision (Value of Information)? 
- Prompts 
  - What assumptions matter most?"
  - What information, if wrong, would change our choice?"
  - Where is our biggest uncertainty?
- Signs of a low score 
  - Reliance on opinion rather than evidence. 
  - Major knowledge gaps. 
  - High confidence despite weak data. 

### Clear Values and Trade-offs 

- Key question: 
  Do we understand what matters most? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Are evaluation criteria explicit? 
  - Have criteria been prioritized? 
  - Are stakeholders aligned on value? 
  - Have trade-offs been discussed openly? 
  - Are hidden objectives influencing the decision? 
  - Is there agreement on risk tolerance? 
- Prompts 
  - What matters most?
  - Which criterion would we sacrifice if necessary?
  - What trade-offs are acceptable?
- Signs of a low score 
  - Endless debate. 
  - Participants use different criteria. 
  - Stakeholders define success differently. 
- Typical trade-offs in industry projects 
  - Value vs. safety 
  - Value vs. emissions 
  - Value vs. schedule 
  - Cost vs. optionality 
  - Short-term vs. long-term value 

### Logically Correct Reasoning

- Key question: 
  Are we using the information correctly? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Is the logic transparent? 
  - Have assumptions been tested? 
  - Are cognitive biases influencing the outcome? 
  - Have uncertainties been incorporated? 
  - Is probabilistic thinking used where appropriate? 
  - Have sensitivities been examined? 
  - Have scenarios been tested? 
  - Is decision robustness understood? 
  - Could reasonable people reach a different conclusion? 
- Prompts 
  - How exactly did we arrive at this conclusion?
  - What are the key assumptions driving the recommendation?
  - What would make us change our view?"
- Signs of a low score 
  - Conclusions unsupported by evidence. 
  - Jumping from data to recommendations. 
  - Failure to explore uncertainty. 

### Commitment to action

- Key question: 
  Can the organization execute the decision? 
- Typical concerns 
  - Are stakeholders aligned? 
  - Are responsibilities clear? 
  - Is there organizational support? 
  - Are resources available? 
  - Are critical risks managed? 
  - Is the decision understood by those who must implement it? 
- Prompts 
  - Can we actually execute this?
  - Who might resist implementation?
  - What would cause execution to fail?
- Signs of a low score 
  - Strong analysis but weak ownership. 
  - No implementation plan. 
  - Lack of stakeholder alignment.
