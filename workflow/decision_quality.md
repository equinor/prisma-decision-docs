# Assess decision quality

## Purpose

Assess and document the quality of the decision-making process. The assessment is not a prediction of whether the eventual outcome will succeed. Evaluate the decision before its outcome is known, using the information available to the team at the time.

Decision quality has six elements. A serious weakness in any one element can limit the quality of the overall decision. Use the assessment to identify whether additional work on an element could change the recommended course of action.

For definitions of the six elements, see [Decision quality](../domain/decision_quality.md).

## How to conduct the assessment

1. Discuss each of the six elements with the decision team.
2. Use the key question and discussion prompts to guide the conversation.
3. Treat the concerns as preparation for the facilitator, not as a checklist that must be completed.
4. Assign a score after discussion, using evidence and the remaining concerns to calibrate it.
5. Record comments that explain the score, important uncertainties, and any further work that could change the decision.

### Overall score anchors

Scores range from 0 to 100. A higher score means fewer or less significant concerns. The values below are anchors; use judgment for scores between them.

| Score | Interpretation |
|-------|----------------|
| 100 | Essentially no material concern. |
| 80 | Minor concerns only. |
| 60 | Several concerns, but not enough to stop the decision. |
| 40 | Important concerns that should make the team cautious. |
| 20 | Serious concerns that undermine confidence. |
| 0 | Fundamentally unacceptable. |

The element-specific descriptions below are illustrative calibration guides. They are not a checklist and do not define a separate scoring formula.

## The six elements

### 1. Appropriate Frame

**Key question:** Are we solving the right decision problem?

**Typical concerns:**
- Is the decision question clearly stated?
- Is success defined?
- Are the decision-makers identified?
- Is the scope too narrow or too broad?
- Have the underlying assumptions been challenged?
- Are we addressing a symptom rather than the root issue?
- Are important stakeholders missing?
- Has the "do nothing" option been considered?

**Discussion prompts:**
- What decision are we actually making?
- If we solve this perfectly, what outcome are we trying to achieve?
- Could we be framing the problem incorrectly?

**Indicators of a low score:**
- Participants disagree about the objective.
- Participants are discussing different decisions.
- Ownership or decision authority is unclear.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | The problem is poorly defined, or stakeholders disagree on the objective. |
| 30-50 | A problem statement exists, but important objectives or constraints are missing. |
| 60-75 | Most stakeholders understand and agree on the main objectives. |
| 80-89 | The decision statement is clear, objectives are explicit, and boundaries are agreed. |
| 90-100 | There is full alignment on scope, objectives, success criteria, and value drivers. |

### 2. Creative, Doable Alternatives

**Key question:** Do we have enough good options to choose from?

**Typical concerns:**
- Are we comparing only variations of the same idea?
- Has the team become attached to a preferred option?
- Have disruptive or unconventional alternatives been considered?
- Have external benchmarks been reviewed?
- Is the status quo included?
- Have hybrid solutions been considered?
- Are implementation issues understood?

**Discussion prompts:**
- What options have we not yet considered?
- If our preferred solution were unavailable, what would we do?
- What would a competitor do?

**Indicators of a low score:**
- Only one realistic option exists.
- The alternatives differ only marginally.
- The decision feels predetermined.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | Only one option is being considered. |
| 30-50 | There are two alternatives, but they are minor variations. |
| 60-75 | Several distinct options have been considered. |
| 80-89 | A broad range of options, including unconventional solutions, has been considered. |
| 90-100 | The alternatives likely span the full opportunity space. |

### 3. Meaningful, Reliable Information

**Key question:** Do we know what we need to know?

**Typical concerns:**
- Are the key uncertainties understood?
- Is critical data missing?
- Are assumptions supported by evidence?
- Is the information current?
- Are the sources trustworthy?
- Have relevant experts been consulted?
- Could unknown information change the decision, and would obtaining it be valuable?

Decision analysis is concerned with uncertainty when resolving it could change the decision. Consider the value of information when deciding whether more information is worth obtaining.

**Discussion prompts:**
- Which assumptions matter most?
- What information, if wrong, would change our choice?
- Where is our greatest uncertainty?

**Indicators of a low score:**
- The decision relies on opinion rather than evidence.
- There are major knowledge gaps.
- Confidence is high despite weak data.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | Data is largely missing or unsupported. |
| 30-50 | Uncertainty is significant and evidence is weak. |
| 60-75 | Technical work is good, but some key uncertainties remain. |
| 80-89 | High-quality data and analysis address the key uncertainties. |
| 90-100 | Information is robust and has been independently challenged. |

### 4. Clear Values and Trade-offs

**Key question:** Do we understand what matters most?

**Typical concerns:**
- Are the evaluation criteria explicit?
- Have the criteria been prioritized?
- Are stakeholders aligned on value?
- Have trade-offs been discussed openly?
- Are hidden objectives influencing the decision?
- Is there agreement on risk tolerance?

**Discussion prompts:**
- What matters most?
- Which criterion would we sacrifice if necessary?
- What trade-offs are acceptable?

Examples of trade-offs in industry projects include value versus safety, value versus emissions, value versus schedule, cost versus optionality, and short-term versus long-term value.

**Indicators of a low score:**
- Debate continues because priorities are unclear.
- Participants use different criteria.
- Stakeholders define success differently.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | Success criteria are undefined. |
| 30-50 | Conflicting objectives remain unresolved. |
| 60-75 | The main priorities are understood. |
| 80-89 | Trade-offs among value drivers are explicit. |
| 90-100 | Value drivers are transparently weighted and decision-makers are aligned. |

### 5. Logically Correct Reasoning

**Key question:** Are we using the information correctly?

**Typical concerns:**
- Is the logic transparent?
- Have assumptions been tested?
- Could cognitive biases be influencing the outcome?
- Have uncertainties been incorporated?
- Is probabilistic thinking used where appropriate?
- Have sensitivities and scenarios been examined?
- Is the robustness of the decision understood?
- Could reasonable people reach a different conclusion?

Influence diagrams and decision trees can help make the reasoning clear. See [Influence diagrams](../domain/influence_diagram.md) and [Decision trees](../domain/decision_tree.md).

**Discussion prompts:**
- How did we arrive at this conclusion?
- Which assumptions drive the recommendation?
- What would make us change our view?

**Indicators of a low score:**
- Conclusions are unsupported by evidence.
- The recommendation does not follow clearly from the analysis.
- Important uncertainty has not been explored.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | Conclusions are based mainly on assumptions or opinions. |
| 30-50 | There are significant gaps in the reasoning. |
| 60-75 | The reasoning is generally sound. |
| 80-89 | There is a clear analytical chain from assumptions to recommendation. |
| 90-100 | The reasoning is rigorous and withstands challenge. |

### 6. Commitment to Action

**Key question:** Can the organization execute the decision?

Decision analysis is iterative. Involving decision-makers in framing the problem and creating alternatives helps build ownership of the decision.

**Typical concerns:**
- Are stakeholders aligned?
- Are responsibilities clear?
- Is there organizational support?
- Are resources available?
- Are critical risks managed?
- Do the people responsible for implementation understand the decision?

**Discussion prompts:**
- Can we execute this decision?
- Who might resist implementation?
- What could cause execution to fail?

**Indicators of a low score:**
- The analysis is strong, but ownership is weak.
- There is no implementation plan.
- Stakeholders are not aligned.

**Illustrative score guide:**

| Score range | Typical situation |
|-------------|-------------------|
| 0-20 | Key stakeholders oppose the decision. |
| 30-50 | There is significant organizational resistance. |
| 60-75 | There is general alignment, but some concerns remain. |
| 80-89 | Stakeholders are committed and resources have been identified. |
| 90-100 | Ownership, alignment, and execution readiness are strong. |

## Using assessments in Prisma

The assessment workflow described here is:

1. A facilitator creates and names an assessment.
2. Project users score the six elements and may add comments.
3. Project users submit their assessments.
4. The assessment is marked complete.
5. Project users can view the evaluation in the group.

### Create an assessment

1. Select **Assessments** in the menu bar.

   <p align="center">
   <img src="./figures/button - evaluation.png" alt="Assessments button in the menu bar." width="60">
   </p>

2. If no assessment exists, select the create-assessment button in the upper-right corner.

   <p align="center">
   <img src="./figures/button - Create Assessment.png" alt="Create Assessment button." width="200">
   </p>

3. Enter a name and select **Add Assessment**.

   <p align="center">
   <img src="./figures/popup - Create Assessment.png" alt="Create Assessment dialog." width="500">
   </p>

### Score and submit

For each element, enter a score from 0 to 100. Add comments to explain the score and any remaining concerns; comments are optional but recommended. When finished, submit the assessment.

<p align="center">
<img src="./figures/window - Create Assessment.png" alt="Decision quality assessment window." width="800">
</p>

### Complete and view the assessment

After assessments have been submitted, mark the assessment as complete. The current workflow documentation does not specify which role can perform this action. Project users can view the evaluation in the group.
